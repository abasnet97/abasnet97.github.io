# My journey into particle physics

It was probably during my seventh or eighth grade that I first learned about the fact that what I thought as the most fundamental building block of matter, i.e. atom, was not so fundamental after all. What John Dalton defined as the "ultimate particle" had a more fundamental structure to it, and the likes of J.J. Thomson and Ernest Rutherford experimentally demonstrated that. I was exposed to a whole new fascinating world of nucleus and electrons. And I probably thought back then "No chance there can be anything more fundamental than that". I had no idea what was waiting for me. But most importantly, I had not realized yet that this intriguing question of "Could there be more fundamental than nucleus?" was going to lead me to an academic path so fascinating.
