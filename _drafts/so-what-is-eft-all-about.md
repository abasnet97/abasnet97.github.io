# So what is Effective Field Theory (EFT) all about?

My first exposure to Effective Field Theory (EFT) was in the summer of 2020 when I had just started working with my PhD supervisor, Dr. Brian Winer, at The Ohio State University. In our first meeting, I remember him saying "My research focuses on using this framework called Effective Field Theory to search for beyond Standard Model physics." And I was left wondering what on earth that was. The reason I was so dumbfounded was primarily because I had no scientific research experience in particle physics, and I had only known one way of looking for new physics, which was to collide particles and look for resonances/bumps in some mass distributions. That was what I remembered people talking about when Higgs boson was discovered in 2012. And I had no idea that a direct production of new particles was not the only way to look for new physics in particle physics lingo. Of course, finding a new particle would be a smoking gun of new physics, but turns out that it is not the only way.

Anyway, I had one job that afternoon after my meeting with Dr. Winer: to understand what an effective field theory is. And after a few hours of google search, I had started making sense of the theoretical idea behind this approach of looking for signs of new physics. In this blog, I am going to give a very general layman's overview of what an effective field theory is. I won't dive into equations at all, and I will try as much as possible to give real life examples and then eventually connect this idea to how this framework is used in particle physics.
